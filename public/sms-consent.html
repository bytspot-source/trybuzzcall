How text consent works

Last updated Oct 22, 2026

Note: this document is a plain-English draft and is pending review by legal counsel. It is not legal advice.

BuzzCall Missed-Call Text-Back is operated by TryBuzzCall for Home Services businesses (plumbing and HVAC) and Braiders. This page describes the program's conservative recorded-permission requirements. It is not a compliance certification or evidence of carrier approval.

Opt-in: written permission before application texts

A missed call is not SMS consent. SMS is off by default. Missed calls, inbound messages, START, payments, waitlist signup, and merely providing a phone number do not create an SMS permission grant. We do not use purchased lists or send marketing messages, and we do not send an unsolicited text to ask for initial opt-in.

1. Review the specific business's disclosure.
Before texting begins, the business identifies itself, its sending number, the nonmarketing message purpose, variable frequency, possible message and data rates, and STOP/HELP instructions. Consent is not a condition of purchase; you may call the business instead.
2. Give written permission outside the automated text flow.
The business collects an affirmative written opt-in, such as a reviewed written or electronic form. It retains the actual evidence securely. Reading this page, calling, or paying is not an opt-in.
3. Record permission for that business, sender, and purpose.
An authenticated operator records the consent time, disclosure version, and a non-secret reference to the evidence. Customer-service permission (customer_service) and owner-alert permission (owner_alerts) are separate. Permission is not transferred to another business or sender.
4. Text only while permission remains active.
Automatic text-back is only for an already permissioned recipient. Every application SMS, including booking/deposit confirmations and owner/billing alerts, passes the permission check. Voice fallback advises you to call again and does not claim that a text was sent.

Twilio's policy permits a response scoped to an incoming text, not general recurring messages. BuzzCall deliberately uses a stricter recorded-written-permission requirement and does not automatically grant permission from an incoming text. Owners and staff must separately give written permission for lead, booking, and account alerts; supplying a setup number is not enough.

HELP, STOP, and starting again

Reply STOP to opt out, HELP for help. Standard opt-out keywords include STOP, STOPALL, UNSUBSCRIBE, CANCEL, END, QUIT, REVOKE, and OPTOUT. Natural-language requests such as "please stop texting me" also revoke all purposes locally, including future confirmations and owner/billing alerts for that recipient. Contact the business or hello@trybuzzcall.com for help without relying on SMS delivery.

Twilio may send a provider-managed opt-out or help response. Its standard filtering is different from optional Advanced Opt-Out, which is disabled by default. If Twilio supplies an OptOutType START, STOP, or HELP notification, it has already replied; BuzzCall does not send a duplicate application reply.

START only clears a delivery block; it never restores grants. After an opt-out, fresh written evidence and a fresh operator-recorded grant are required. If Twilio has blocked delivery, you must first explicitly opt back in with the provider; that step alone does not restore BuzzCall permission. We do not promise an application confirmation after revocation, because application texts remain blocked.

Program details

  • Program name: BuzzCall Missed-Call Text-Back (TryBuzzCall)
  • Message types: nonmarketing missed-call follow-ups, appointment options, booking and deposit links, and confirmations covered by customer-service permission. Separately permissioned owners receive lead, booking, and billing/account alerts. Payment or an existing booking does not override an opt-out.
  • Message frequency varies. Message and data rates may apply.
  • Reply STOP to opt out, HELP for help. Carriers are not liable for delayed or undelivered messages.
  • Consent to receive texts is not a condition of purchase.
  • Mobile numbers and SMS opt-in data are not shared with or sold to third parties or affiliates for marketing purposes. See our Privacy Policy and SMS Terms.

Provider sources and review boundary

These sources describe provider requirements, not approval of a particular business or legal certification of this draft:

  • Twilio Messaging Policy (April 13, 2026 version): prior express consent, proof, written consent for downstream software customers, limited inbound-message scope, and no transfer of consent.
  • ISV onboarding and A2P 10DLC quickstart: actual customer identity, verified campaign and associated sender, and no unsolicited SMS to obtain initial permission. Registration does not replace recipient consent.
  • Advanced Opt-Out: provider filtering and reply handling. Local permission records remain separate.
