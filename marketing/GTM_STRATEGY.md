## 0. Executive summary

1. **Don't sell text-back.** It's built into GoHighLevel, Podium, Birdeye, Weave, Jobber, Housecall Pro and ServiceTitan. Sell **booked, paid jobs**: "$49 deposit in your account before you're off the ladder."
2. **Who to sell to:** Georgia plumbing and HVAC shops with 1–5 trucks that schedule by phone, paper or a calendar, *not* field-service software. Start with plumbers in the suburbs and small towns, not intown Atlanta HVAC, where BookedBack is already selling.
3. **Offer:** free 14-day pilot, then $49/mo plus $12 only when a caller books *and* pays. Add a suggested **"Paid job in 30 days or the month is free"** guarantee. No one else in the research pairs a guarantee with pay-per-paid-lead pricing.
4. **Channel #1, the after-hours missed-call demo:** call the shop after hours, note that nobody picked up and nothing texted back, then show them on *their own* phone what their caller would have gotten. It's free, it's proof, and it's the product doing its own pitch.
5. **Channel #2, in person:** supply-house counters at 6:30–8am, PHCC of Georgia meetings and CE classes. **Channel #3:** a referral and partner program that turns pilots into introductions.
6. **90-day goals:** 400 shops contacted, 15–18 pilots, 10 paying shops, 2–3 real case studies, all for **under $500** (planned spend about $450, est.).
7. **Biggest risks to manage up front:** TCPA (don't cold-text prospects), A2P 10DLC registration time and per-shop cost, the "keep your number" claim (texts may come from a new local number), and seasonal churn.
8. **Fix before you contact anyone:** the site, pitch sheet and outreach kit still say **$79–149/mo + $99 setup** and use the "bytspot" name. Change them to the $49 + $12 model and the TryBuzzCall/BuzzCall brand.

## 1. Positioning, core message, offer

**One-line positioning**
> BuzzCall turns the calls you miss into booked, paid jobs for Georgia plumbers and HVAC shops. We set it up for you, you keep your number, and there's no app.

**Core message (the one sentence every asset lands on)**
> **"$49 deposit in your account before you're off the ladder."**

**Three proof points (always in this order)**
1. **Booked + paid, not just texted.** The caller picks one of 3 open slots and pays a $49 deposit, credited to their bill. Your phone buzzes: **BOOKED + PAID**, with urgent jobs flagged.
2. **Done for you.** Same number for callers, plain text messages, no app or dashboard. We handle setup and the carrier texting registration.
3. **Local.** A real person in Georgia, in-person setup, and ready-made scripts for heat waves (AC out), cold snaps (no heat) and freezes (burst pipes).

**What we never lead with:** "missed-call text-back," "AI," or competitor names. Text-back is the plumbing; the deliverable is a paid booking.

### The offer

| Piece | What it is | Why it beats the field |
|---|---|---|
| **Free 14-day pilot** | No card to start. Live on their line, real callers. | Matches or beats rivals' trials. CatchMissed charges $149 setup. |
| **$49/mo base** | Same as Operator's starting price. | Price parity with the closest rival, much lower than $79–149 rivals. |
| **+$12 per paid lead** | Charged **only** when a caller books **and** the deposit clears. | "You only pay extra after you've been paid." That's rare in this market. |
| **Setup waived on annual** | Setup fee waived with a 12-month commitment. | Rewards commitment and cuts winter churn. |
| **Guarantee (suggestion)** | **"Paid job in 30 days or the month is free."** If BuzzCall doesn't produce at least one paid booking in a shop's first paid 30 days, refund or skip that $49. | Takes away the last bit of risk. It costs you little (see below). |
| **Founding Shop deal (suggestion)** | First 10 paying shops lock $49/mo for life in exchange for a case study and a reference call. | Buys proof cheaply and creates urgency ("10 spots"). |

*Why the guarantee is cheap for you (est.):* if a shop gets zero paid bookings, you've lost one $49 month. Those are the same shops that would churn anyway, so the guarantee mostly costs you revenue you wouldn't have kept. Put it in writing with a simple definition: "paid booking = a caller deposit that cleared and wasn't refunded because the shop cancelled."

*Optional fear reducer (suggestion):* cap lead fees per month (example: no more than 15 × $12 = $180). Most small shops won't hit the cap, and it answers "what if it gets expensive?"

## 2. Ideal customer profile (ICP)

**Best fit (score 1 point each; pursue at 4+):**
- [ ] Plumbing or HVAC, licensed in Georgia (verify on the SOS license lookup)
- [ ] 1–5 trucks; the owner still runs calls or answers the phone
- [ ] Schedules by phone, paper, Google Calendar or a whiteboard. **Not** on Jobber, Housecall Pro or ServiceTitan.
- [ ] Missed your after-hours test call **and** sent no text-back
- [ ] Owner's cell is the business line, or calls ring to one person
- [ ] Does emergency or same-day work (water heaters, leaks, no-heat, no-AC)
- [ ] Google Business Profile with reviews but no online booking button

**Poor fit (skip or deprioritize):**
- Already on field-service software. They can turn on built-in text-back (Jobber sells it as a $29 add-on).
- Has an answering service or office staff answering live during business hours.
- 10+ trucks with a dispatcher (a different buyer, longer sale).
- New construction-only or commercial-only shops (few homeowner emergency calls).

**Where to start geographically:** plumbers in metro suburbs outside the core city (Cobb, Gwinnett, Cherokee, Henry, Douglas, Paulding) and mid-size Georgia towns you can drive to (Macon, Athens, Gainesville). Reason: BookedBack is positioned on Atlanta HVAC. Go where small shops are thick and attention is less crowded. Add HVAC in the same suburbs once you have one plumbing case study.

**Persona:** "Darnell, owner of a 2-truck plumbing shop in Marietta." Answers his own cell. Under a house from 8 to 5. Calls back voicemails at night, and some of those callers have already hired someone else. Hates software and contracts. Trusts other plumbers, his supply-house counter guy, and people who show up in person.

### 3.1 Channel #1: the after-hours "missed-call demo"

**The idea:** you become their missed caller. Then you show them exactly what that caller should have gotten, using a live demo line.

**Prerequisite: a working demo line.** A Twilio number (upgraded from trial, 10DLC-registered under TryBuzzCall) set up as a sample shop, e.g. "Peach State Plumbing (BuzzCall demo)". The prospect calls it, lets it ring out, gets the text, picks a slot, and pays a **Stripe test-mode** deposit.
*Product tweak to request (suggestion):* a demo mode that sends the "BOOKED + PAID" buzz to the **prospect's own phone** too, so they feel both sides in 60 seconds. Because the prospect calls or texts the demo line first, replying to them is a response, not a cold text.

**Step-by-step**

1. **Build a batch** of 30–40 shops from Google Maps (§2) every Monday.
2. **Make the test call after hours but inside normal calling hours:** weekdays **6:00–8:45pm local**, or Saturdays 9–11am. (Staying between 8am and 9pm is the federal TCPA telemarketing window. Respect it even when calling businesses.) Dial manually from your own phone. No autodialer, no robocalls.
3. **Log the result:** answered / voicemail / full mailbox / text-back received (and how many minutes later). Don't leave a sales voicemail on this first call; it's a test.
    - If they **answered**: give the 20-second pitch (script below) or ask for a better time.
    - If they **already have text-back**: lower priority. The pitch becomes "you're texting them back; are they booking and paying?"
4. **Next business day, reach them a different way.** The best windows (est.) are 7:00–7:45am, before trucks roll, or 12:00–1:00pm. Call, email if the website lists one, or walk in if they're nearby.
5. **The reveal script (phone, about 45 seconds):**
   > "Hey {first}, {you} with BuzzCall, here in Georgia. Quick one: I called {shop} last night at {time}. It went to voicemail and nothing came back. Not a knock, you were off the clock. But a homeowner with a leak would've called the next name on Google.
   > Want to see what they'd have gotten instead? Call this number right now and just let it ring: **[demo line]**. You'll get a text, pick a time, and see the 'BOOKED + PAID' alert. Takes a minute. If you like it, I'll set it up on your line free for 14 days."
6. **Voicemail version (only on the follow-up call, 20 seconds):**
   > "{first}, {you} with BuzzCall in Georgia. I called your shop last night at {time} and it went to voicemail, which is exactly when customers drift to the next plumber. Call **[demo line]** and let it ring to see what your callers could get: a text, a booked slot, and a $49 deposit. My cell is [your phone]. Thanks."
7. **Email version** (if a public business email is listed). Subject: *"I called {shop} at {time} last night"*. Body: the reveal script, the demo line, the pilot offer, and an opt-out line ("Reply 'no thanks' and I won't email again").
8. **Cadence:** Day 0 test call → Day 1 reveal call/email → Day 3 walk-in if within 20 minutes, or a 2nd call → Day 7 last call. Then move them to a quarterly check-in list. **Text them only if they've texted you, called the demo line, or said "text me."**
9. **Close:** "I just need your cell, your hours, and 20 minutes to set it up. In person if you like." Book the setup on the spot.

**Bonus content from this channel (real data you generate):** after 50+ test calls, publish **"We called 50 Cobb County plumbers after 6pm. Here's what happened."** Report only your own counts (answered / voicemail / text-back), plus how you sampled and when. Label it as your own small sample, not an industry stat. That's local PR, a Facebook post, and a sales opener in one.

### 3.2 Channel #2: in person (supply houses + trade groups)

**Supply houses (weekly, Tue–Thu)**
- *Targets:* plumbing and HVAC distributor branches near your target counties (e.g., Ferguson, Winsupply, Johnstone Supply, Shearer Supply, Carrier Enterprise, local independents). Confirm addresses on Google Maps.
- *When:* **6:30–8:00am**, when trucks load up (est.; ask the counter staff when it's busiest and when it's quiet).
- *Playbook:*
    1. **Visit 1, the branch manager, during a quiet hour.** Not a pitch, a favor: "I help small plumbing shops stop losing after-hours calls. Could I put a sheet on your board? Which of your small accounts can never pick up?" Bring a dozen donuts (about $15, est.).
    2. **Leave 10 pitch sheets** at the counter, with a QR code to the demo line and your cell.
    3. **Visit 2+, at the morning rush.** Stand *outside* or by the coffee, never blocking the counter. Ask one question: "How many calls do you miss while you're on a job?" Hand the sheet only if they engage.
    4. **Offer the branch something:** "If any of your accounts sign up, I'll tell them you sent them." (Supply houses like being useful to their contractors.) Later this can become a formal partner (§3.3).
- *Goal:* 2 supply-house branches per week, same ones repeatedly. Familiarity is the point.

### 3.3 Channel #3: referral & partner program

**Customer referrals: "Buzz a buddy"** (start the day a pilot sees its first paid booking)
- Ask at the **moment of delight**, right after the first BOOKED + PAID alert: "Who's another plumber or HVAC guy you'd want to have this?"
- **Reward (suggestion):** referrer gets **one free month** ($49 credit) when the referred shop's first paid month clears. The referred shop gets the guarantee plus an extended 21-day pilot. Cash is tight, so pay in credit, not cash.
- Plumbers often know HVAC owners (and vice versa) who aren't competitors. Ask for the *other* trade.
- Script: "Glad it's working. Most of my shops come from guys like you. Know one plumber or HVAC owner who misses calls? I'll set him up free and give you next month free if he sticks."

**Partners (people who already have small-shop owners' trust)**

| Partner type | What they get (suggestion) | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| Bookkeepers / tax preparers for trades | $50 per shop that pays 2 months, or 15% of the base for 12 months | "When clients complain about missed calls, hand them this." |
| Web designers / Google Business Profile & SEO freelancers | Same, or a co-branded "booking upgrade" they can resell | Bundle with site builds. They drive calls, you convert them. |
| Supply-house branch staff | Recognition plus coffee or lunch for the counter. Ask about company gift rules first. | Flyer on the board, warm intros |
| Contractor insurance agents | Same as bookkeepers | Intro email to their trade clients |
| Non-competing trades (electricians, garage door, appliance repair) | Free month per referral | Swap intros. Also future verticals. |

- Pay partner rewards only **after the shop's second paid month** (protects cash and filters bad referrals).
- Keep one simple tracker: partner · referred shop · date · status · reward owed.
- Ready-made partner kit: one-page sheet, demo line, a forwardable email, and a link with `?ref=partnername`.

## 7. Proof engine: pilots → case studies → recaps

**Before the pilot (baseline)**
- Ask: "Roughly how many calls a week do you miss? What's an average service call worth? What's the most you've lost from one missed call?" Write down *their* answers and label them owner estimates.
- Get written permission in the pilot agreement to use first name, shop name, city and anonymized results. Separate checkbox for photo/video.

**During the pilot (instrumentation)**
- From logs: missed calls caught, replies, bookings, deposits paid, urgent flags, "callback" requests, STOPs, owner callback time.
- **Day 3 check-in** (5-minute call): "Anything sound off in the wording?"
- **Screenshot the first BOOKED + PAID alert** (blur the caller's name and number). This is your best asset.

**Day 14 wrap (the close)**
- Send the pilot recap text (existing template), then sit down in person: "Here's what it caught, here's what it booked, here's what's already in your Stripe." Close on the guarantee and the Founding Shop rate.

**Case study template (one page, 150 words, real numbers only)**

## 8. Pricing psychology & ROI

**ROI one-liner (illustration):**
> **"One recovered $300 job pays for about 6 months of BuzzCall."**
> *Math, illustration only:* $300 job − its $12 lead fee = $288; $288 ÷ $49/mo ≈ 5.9 months. $300 is an example job value, not a measured average. Use the owner's own average ticket in conversation.

**Tactics**
1. **Anchor on the job, not the software.** Always compare $49 with one service call, never with other apps.
2. **"You pay $12 after you've already been paid $49."** The lead fee comes out of money the deposit already put in their pocket. That's the most persuasive line in the model.
3. **Same number as the deposit.** "$49 a month, the same as one deposit." Easy to remember.
4. **Risk reversal stack:** free 14 days → paid job in 30 days or the month is free (suggestion) → no contract on monthly.
5. **Annual = setup waived.** Pitch in January ("lock it in before AC season"), never during the pilot.
6. **Scarcity that's true:** "10 Founding Shop spots." Show the real remaining count.
7. **Simplicity:** one plan. No tiers until 20+ shops ask for something different.
8. **Lead-fee cap** (suggestion) to answer "what if it gets expensive?"

**Decide before launch:**
- Who pays Stripe processing on the $49 deposit? Recommend the shop (it's their revenue), disclosed up front.
- Define "paid lead" in writing (deposit cleared, not refunded because of shop cancellation, counted once per job).
- **Retire the old pricing:** the site, pitch sheet and `outreach_texts.md` say "$79–149/mo, setup from $99," and `docs/OUTREACH_SMS.md` #4 says "from $79/mo." Update all of them to $49/mo + $12 per paid lead.

## 10. How to beat competitors

| Competitor type | Their strength | How BuzzCall wins | Don't |
|---|---|---|---|
| Built-in text-back (GoHighLevel agencies, Podium, Birdeye, Weave) | Bundled, cheap or "free" in a suite | They send a text; you deliver a **paid booking** and only charge extra when it happens. No suite, no contract, no dashboard. | Compete on features or price per text. |
| Field-service software (Jobber $29 add-on, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan) | Already in the shop's workflow | **Don't fight.** Target shops *not* on these platforms. | Try to replace their software. |
| Operator (from $49/mo, text-back + booking + Stripe deposit) | Closest product, same entry price | Pay-per-paid-lead, guarantee, **in-person Georgia setup**, local emergency scripts, a human you can call. | Assume the product alone differentiates you. |
| BookedBack (targets Atlanta HVAC) | Local focus on HVAC | Lead with **plumbing** and suburbs/small GA towns. Beat them on presence (supply houses, PHCC). | Start a price war in intown HVAC. |
| CatchMissed ($79 + $149 setup), BeepBack ($79), TextedBack ($99–149), Truckbell | Simple text-back | Lower base price, no setup on annual, deposit + booking, guarantee. | Name them to prospects. |
| AI receptionists (Rosie, Goodcall, Smith.ai, $0–299) | Answer live with voice | "No robot on your phone": plain texts in your voice. The caller books and pays, and you only pay when they do. | Claim AI superiority. |

**Moats you can build in 90 days:** (1) in-person relationships in 2–3 counties, (2) real local case studies, (3) Georgia weather scripts and timing, (4) partner network, (5) the pay-per-paid-lead promise. None of these depends on features a big platform can copy in a sprint.

## 11. Risks & how to handle them

| Risk | What could go wrong | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **TCPA (your own sales outreach)** | Cold texts to owners' cell phones without consent can bring legal claims. Many small-shop "business lines" are personal cells. | **Don't cold-text prospects.** Call (manually, 8am–9pm local), email, or walk in. Text only after they text you, call the demo line, or say "text me," and log that consent. Keep an internal do-not-contact list and honor it immediately. **Revise `outreach_texts.md` §1 ("Cold SMS")** so it's used only with prior consent or a relationship. Get a one-time review from a TCPA-savvy attorney before scaling. (This is not legal advice.) |
| **TCPA / content (the product's texts)** | Text-backs that drift into marketing, or texts to people who didn't call. | Text only numbers that just called the shop. Keep messages transactional (booking). Include STOP/HELP. Rely on Twilio Advanced Opt-Out. Never add callers to promo lists. |
| **A2P 10DLC** | Registration delays (Twilio estimates under a week, sometimes longer), rejection, a per-shop cost of ~$20 (est.), and campaign suspension if a campaign is used outside its registered purpose (e.g., sales outreach on a customer-care campaign). | Register early. Match the campaign description to the real flow. Link to `sms-consent.html`, privacy and terms pages that are live on HTTPS. Keep one campaign per real use case. Never send prospecting texts through client campaigns. |
| **"Keep your number" claim** | With no-answer call forwarding, callers dial the shop's number, but the **text-back may come from a new BuzzCall local number**, not the shop's own number. The current kit says "a text from your number." | Say it precisely: "Customers still call your same number; the text comes from a local number set up for your shop." Or look into Twilio hosted SMS on the shop's existing number as an upgrade. Fix this wording before launch. |
| **Deposit friction** | Some callers won't pay up front, so bookings drop. | Keep the "callback" option. Track reply → deposit drop-off. Test a lower deposit or a "no deposit for existing customers" option if drop-off is high. |
| **Refunds / chargebacks** | Disputes on deposits; unclear who owns refunds. | Publish the refund policy (draft exists; attorney review). The shop's Stripe account takes the deposit. Waive the $12 when a deposit is refunded because of the shop. |
| **Churn (seasonality)** | Shops cancel in slow months or after one bad week. | Annual (setup waived), weekly recaps, monthly "money found" texts, seasonal scripts (freeze, no heat, heat wave), quarterly visits. |
| **Founder bandwidth** | Setup + support + sales by one person. | A setup checklist (exists), templated wording, setup limited to 20 minutes, batched setups on Fridays. Cap pilots at 4 live at once until the process is smooth. |
| **Product reliability** | Single-tenant JSON store, no retry queue: a missed buzz breaks trust. | Before pilot #3: multi-tenant config, error alerts to your phone, uptime check on `/health`, a daily "all good" self-test. |
| **Copycats / price drops** | Operator or GHL agencies match the pricing. | Compete on presence, proof and guarantee, not price. |
| **Brand confusion** | Assets say "bytspot"; the brand is TryBuzzCall/BuzzCall. | Use "BuzzCall (TryBuzzCall)" on every asset. |
